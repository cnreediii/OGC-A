## Summary of SIDP Initiative

In 2003 the Open GIS Consortium, Inc – Australia (OGC – A) and ASIBA were engaged by the AusIndustry Innovation Access Program and Industry to realise a series of example scenarios that demonstrate how interoperable spatial data and processing services can aid in improving management and communications with the industry and communities of interest in public/civil emergencies and critical incident management such as bushfires.

## Budget

AusIndustry provided a project budget of AU $2,104,720 over a twelve month period. The participants agreed to an equivalent $ amount in inkind services and infrastructure. The total of funded $$ and inkind $$ would be approximately US $5,375,945.71 in todays dollars. This is perhaps the largest OGC Interoperability Initiative ever!

## Summary of Project

The IDP will demonstrate and promote interoperability and OpenGIS® and related ISO standards and protocols that will ‘geo-enable’ a range of web, wireless and location-based services, and mainstream IT. It will lead to a greater application of international open standards for spatial information processing in Australian industry and Government providing ubiquitous accessibility over the Internet. 

The project is a response to demands from spatial businesses (represented by ASIBA), their customers (exemplified by the Dept of Defence, Insurance Industry Association and the Retail Traders Association) and users (for example, businesses affiliated with the industry associations and include volunteers, through the Volunteer Firefighters Assoc and Surf Life Saving Australia).  

The demand (“user pull”) is to have map data available over the internet, to reduce costs (start-up and operating), increase accessibility to map and map related information  meeting Governments’(NOIE Interoperability Projects 2002-03), businesses’ (Australian Business News 2001) and public good requirements (McLeod Inquiry 2003).

The market demands cn will be met by providing skills transfer and capacity building within SMEs and providing for process and technology knowledge by demonstrating and promoting interoperability through OpenGIS® and related standards and protocols. 

The project will establish a basis for the stabilising and growth of the spatial industry in Australia, in much the same way that the adoption of common standards and procedures enabled the Internet to develop.  Search engines on the Internet are a prime example of interoperability.  Web pages are composed of textual, numeric and pictorial data and created the need for Standard Languages (for example, .html (text) and .jpg (images)) accessible via a standard protocol (Internet Protocol IP).  This allows data from from anywhere to be accessed, indexed, searched and made available.  Spatial data, is just another data type, that can be described by a standard language and accessible by a standard protocol..  Not all content over the internet is free, just as all map data is not free, the IDP will also show how standards based strategies for charging for information can be delivered with models for revenue generation.
